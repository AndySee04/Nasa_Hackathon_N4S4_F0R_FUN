The Sun is a star: a hot, glowing ball of hydrogen and helium at the center of our solar system. It is about 4.5 billion years old, and scientists call it a yellow dwarf star.

The Sun is about 150 million km from Earth. It is so big that it would take about 1.3 million Earths to fill it, and more than 330,000 Earths to match its mass.

The hottest part of the Sun is its core, where hydrogen is squeezed together to make helium. This process, called nuclear fusion, makes the Sun's heat and light. The core is about 15 million °C. The visible surface, called the photosphere, is much cooler at about 5,500 °C.

The Sun is not solid, so different parts spin at different speeds. At its equator it spins once about every 25 Earth days, but near its poles it takes about 36 days.

The Sun has no moons of its own, but all the planets and their moons travel around it.

Never look straight at the Sun, even with sunglasses. It can hurt your eyes.

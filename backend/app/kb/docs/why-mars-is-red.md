Mars is called the Red Planet because its rocks, soil and dust contain lots of iron. When iron is exposed to oxygen, it oxidizes, or rusts, and turns reddish, just like an old bike left outside.

Wind lifts this rusty dust into the air. From far away, the dust makes the whole planet look red, and from the ground it can make the Martian sky look pink.

Up close, rovers show that Mars is really many colors. Much of it looks butterscotch, and some places look golden, brown, tan or even a little greenish, depending on the minerals there.

The ancient Romans named Mars after their god of war because its color reminded them of blood. The ancient Egyptians called it "Her Desher", which means "the red one".

Saturn is not the only planet with rings (Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune have them too), but Saturn's are the biggest and brightest.

The rings are not solid. They are made of billions of chunks of water ice and rock, coated with dust. Most pieces range from tiny icy grains to chunks as big as a house, and a few are as big as mountains.

Even though the rings are enormous, they are very thin: generally only about 10 meters thick.

Scientists think the rings are pieces of comets, asteroids or shattered moons that were torn apart by Saturn's strong gravity before they reached the planet.

NASA's Cassini spacecraft studied the rings up close and spotted streaks called "spokes" that can be longer than Earth is wide. Scientists think spokes are tiny icy grains lifted by electric charge, like hair standing up near a rubbed balloon.
